## Slide text

- slide_01: How to write the Stanford Essays
- slide_02: Q1: The Stanford community is deeply curious and driven to learn in and out of the classroom. Reflect on an idea or experience that makes you genuinely excited about learning. (100-250 words) ¢ Talk about something you learned that surprised you when you learned it. Can pick something narrow ¢ Ivy Brothers says: Show the rabbit hole. This means, show how you became interested in this from the classroom to conversations to projects ¢ Remember: Stanford likes students who describe the process (iteration) and they like intellectual wandering, tinkering, etc.
- slide_03: Q2: Virtually all of Stanford's undergraduates live on campus. Write a note to your future roommate that reveals something about you or that will help your roommate—and us—get to know you better. ¢ Ivy Brothers says: Make this sound endearing but also quirky ¢ For example, one of our students talked about how she likes to knit fabric puppets of her friends ¢ Find something that’s going to stand out
- slide_04: Q3: Please describe what aspects of your life experiences, interests and character would help you make a distinctive contribution as an undergraduate to Stanford University. For this one, Ivy Brothers suggests talking about a pattern that shows up in your behavior. Then, back it up with 1-2 real life experiences Lastly, talk about how this pattern will help you make a contribution to Stanford For example, you could be the translator, the mediator, etc.
- slide_05: Q1 (Short): What is the most significant challenge society faces today? e Answer with just a couple words or phrases about the challenge and then explain ¢ Stanford wants to see “systems” level thinking, not activism feel-good language e Avoid generic topics (like climate change or the ethics of Al)
- slide_06: Q2 (Short): How did you spend your last two summers? ¢ Ivy Brothers suggests the format of: © Summer 1, I worked on XYZ problem o Summer 2 , | upped the stakes or evolved my approach ¢ Red flag for this prompt is dropping a laundry list of resumé bullet points
- slide_07: Q3 (Short): What historical moment or event do you wish you could have witnessed? ¢ Ivy Brothers says: Pick a time in history that relates to your spike ¢ For example, if you are interested in biotechnology, you should talk about a historical event like the first prosthetic leg being invented
- slide_08: Q4 (Short): Briefly elaborate on one of your extracurricular activities, a job you hold, or responsibilities you have for your family. Focus on the lesson or takeaway Discuss what’s not covered in the rest of you application or dive deeper on one of your biggest ECs Again, Stanford likes systems or process level thinking Ivy Brothers suggests the format: Problem — what you tried at first > iteration/pivot > lesson that you learned
- slide_09: Q5 (Short): List five things that are important to you. For this prompt, filter for the things Stanford is looking for: agency (you chose it, not adults), specificity (narrow and personal), unfinished tension (you're still working on it), process over outcome, and innovation. To help you brainstorm: ask some of these question: What do I do even when no one is watching? What do I get annoyed by? If you say things like curiosity or lifelong learning, you failed this question Optimize for quirky or weirdness here; Stanford is looking for this Describe a scene (start each sentence with a verb)
