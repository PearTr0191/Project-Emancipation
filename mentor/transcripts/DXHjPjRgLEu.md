- slide_02: Got rejected? Do this next (Appeal Letter)
- slide_03: What to know about appeals *Less than 1% of rejected applicants ever submit an appeal letter Of those who do, most write emotional pleas about how much they love the school That doesn't work unless you're emphasizing a legacy connection. Admissions officers already know your kid wants to attend. That's not new information The families who actually overturn decisions approach appeals like a legal case
- slide_04: What to include (part 1) An effective appeal letter does one thing: introduces materially new information that wasn't in the original application That means a major award received after submission, a significant new achievement, a new letter of support from someone with real weight It can even be an independent research topic that you started working on, and are excited to continue at XYZ University.
- slide_05: What else to include (part 2) If possible, a letter from a professor they've built a relationship with saying "I want this student in my program.” This carries enormous internal influence We recommend auditing a class on campus or sitting in on a lecture and mentioning your plan on how you will contribute to either this class or the subject matter.
- slide_06: Last Tips * Timing matters. Submit within 1-2 weeks of the decision. After that, the committee has mentally moved on Schools like Emory, Vanderbilt, Tulane, and UVA have historically been more receptive to appeals i than most families realize The worst outcome is they say no again. The best outcome is a life- changing reversal that happened because you sent one letter most people never bother writing
