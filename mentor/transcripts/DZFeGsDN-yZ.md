## Caption

This year I had the honor of being selected as the 2026 Undergraduate Student Speaker for Harvard’s Pan-Asian AAPI Graduation Ceremony.

I decided to surprise my dad, who had no idea I was giving this speech, let alone dedicate it to him and my mother.

My parents sacrificed their education so I could have mine, and no words can ever thank them enough.

Now, I choose to carry that gift forward by helping other students access opportunities that can transform their futures, just as education transformed mine ❤️

## Transcript

In 1993 my dad fled Vietnam with sunburns from helping his mother sell produce at the market and stories about building cricket traps to catch dinner. He arrived with nothing in his pockets, no English, no degree, no one waiting for him at the airport. For 10 years he scrubbed toilets and worked two fast food and janitor jobs to help make rent for the 400 square foot apartment he shared with his mom, dad and two other siblings. He learned a country one humiliating sentence at a time and kept every receipt in a shoebox under his bed because when you've already lost everything once you do not throw anything away that proves you exist. I am standing here today because of him and now dad I would like for you to stand up to be recognized as well. I'm also standing here today because of my mom who passed from cancer before she could see what this country could give her daughters. I remember her in the last week of her life telling me with the half of her face that wasn't yet paralyzed to live the life she would not get to. I came to Harvard carrying my father's last name and mother's unfinished sentences and no idea what to do with either. Most of what I know about being AAPI I learned in rooms my parents would never get the chance to walk into. Monday nights in Quincy private dining room with Harvard's organization of Asian American sisters in service talking about how our mothers would tell us I love you through cut fruit. Saturday morning shifts at Zuchi Chinatown community fridge where grandmothers I could not speak a sentence to rolled their carts up to mine and made me somebody's granddaughter. Three years in the Harvard Vietnamese language program learning my father's native language the one I grew up two Americans to speak until I could ask my grandmother how she really was and not just whether she had eaten. My dad kept every receipt to prove he existed. I came to Harvard thinking I would have to do the same. We do not. Class of 2026 look at the person sitting next by you. They are somebody's entire life and so are you. Our families did not get us this far to keep being smaller than we are. Apologize for nothing. Lead in the rooms they were not let into and cut fruit for the next AAPI kid the same way our mothers cut it for us. Class of 2026 stand proud make your voice heard and finish the sentences they could not. Thank you.
