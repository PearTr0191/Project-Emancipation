## Slides (OCR)

Most people think getting accepted into Ivy
Leagues is about "good grades"
That's what I thought too...
Until I spoke to admissions counselors about
what *actually* matters.
That shaped my entire strategy for getting
into these schools.

---

#1- Grades, SAT & ACT
These matter...
But not as much as you'd think.
Every person that applies has these at a
BAREMINIMUM.
They're required to get accepted but won't
make too much of a difference in getting
accepted.

---

#2 - Extracurriculars
Colleges don't care about how many you do
as much as they care about...
- Your story
- Your passion
- You wanting to improve the world
1 extracurricular that you truly believe in and
can be passionate about is worth 20+
random activities.

---

#3 - College Choice
I'm not going to lie...
I applied to tons of colleges I knew I didn't
want to go to.
I studied the best colleges for whatI was
studying for and judged them on...
- Work Environment
- Studies Ranking
- Would I Be Proud Being an Alumni
I could've crossed out every college that
wasn'tan8/10onalloftheseandbeen
perfectly fine

---

#4 - Scholarships
There's more scholarships than people know
what to do with these days...
I used careeronestop.org and
accessscholarships.com to find more
"hidden" applications where...
- Less people are applying
-Awardsare$500-$5,000
- Based on niche activities/communities
And ALWAYS apply to FAFSA early.

---

This is how I would tackle every important
field individually.
To make them flow coherently...
I would predetermine my schedule at the
beginning of the year so I'm spending quality
time on each.

---

Spend 5 hours every Sunday studying.
Spend 3 hours every Thursday on financial
aid.
Spend 2hours afterschool onMondays
researching colleges.
The more time you carve out on your
schedule the more you'll be prepared.

---

Don't forget...
SAT registration closes August 28.
FAFSA applications start on October 1.
Early Action apps are due on November 1.
Prepare now so you don't get burnt out when
the time comes.
