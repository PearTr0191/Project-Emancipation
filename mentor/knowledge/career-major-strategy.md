# Career & Major Strategy (AI Era) — from American Study panel transcript

> Source: Vietnamese-language panel talk by consultancy "American Study" (pasted 2026-08-26, not yet in `transcripts/`). Consultancy marketing content — treat every number as directional until verified against BLS/employer data. Trust labels: [solid] / [directional] / [inflated].

## The one durable idea: jobs split, they don't vanish

AI absorbs the routine-analysis layer of a job; humans keep accountability, judgment, and real-world interaction. So the question isn't "will my major disappear" but "which half of it does AI take." Examples given:
- Doctor: AI reads scans and drafts diagnoses; human keeps treatment decisions and legal responsibility.
- Finance: AI builds reports (Salesforce/Tableau anecdote: 2-month report → 1 week); humans move to investment strategy, tax, negotiation.
- Software: AI writes routine code; humans keep system architecture and directing AI teams.

[solid] as a framing device — matches what the student already lives: her three products are data applied to domains, not data for its own sake.

## Field direction (2030s horizon)

Rising: AI/ML engineering, data engineering, cybersecurity, robotics + semiconductors, biomedical/health-tech engineering, quantitative finance, energy tech, materials science.
Declining as standalone careers: basic accounting/audit, data entry, low-level financial analysis, scan reading/radiology support, translation, basic content marketing, routine driving.

[directional] — consistent with Khosla's public predictions ("no drivers, no programmers, no accountants" by 2030) but nobody can date this precisely. Use for essay/interview framing, not for life decisions.

**Implication for this student:** DS/AI-ML is on the right side of the split, but pure tool skills commoditize fastest. Pair the tool with a domain (her through-line already does: cars → decisions, rescue → emergencies, museum → memory). This sharpens "why this major": not "I love data," but "data only pays off when pointed at a domain."

## Interdisciplinary convergence

US schools push double majors and cross-school enrollment at no extra cost (liberal-arts model). Named programs usable as why-us ammunition:
- Stanford d.school / "Design Your Life" course
- Brown Open Curriculum (no core requirements)
- Musk at UPenn: physics + Wharton simultaneously → funded PayPal-era startup
- Steve Jobs' Reed calligraphy course → Mac typography

[solid] that these programs exist; [cliché] if used verbatim in essays — AOs have read the Jobs story thousands of times. Mine school-specific versions instead when writing supplements.

## Visa mechanics (supports the existing employment→green-card lens)

STEM OPT = 36 months of post-study work; H-1B lottery can be entered each year while on OPT (~3 attempts typical). PhD in the US is fully funded plus stipend — a zero-cost continuation path if grades hold. [solid]

## What to REJECT from this transcript

1. **Aid conflation — the dangerous one.** The talk claims GPA 9+ / SAT ~1450 unlocks "100% financial aid." False mechanism: at meets-full-need schools, aid size comes from family finances, not stats. Stats buy admission; the aid formula then computes need. Merit scholarships for internationals exist but full rides are rare and concentrated in the need-blind + meets-full-need set (Harvard, MIT, Princeton, Yale, Amherst, Dartmouth). "Học bổng toàn phần không khó" is sales copy.
2. **Fixed admission weights (SAT 20% / GPA 20% / EC 30% / essays 20% / recs-interview 10%).** Fabricated precision; contradicts holistic review (see myths-red-flags.md). Directional echo only: ECs ≈ academics > essays > recs.
3. **GPA-to-tier mapping (9.0 → top 10–20; 8.5 → top 50–100).** Roughly true directionally; irrelevant here since the student sits at 9.5–9.7.
