# Interviews

## The core insight: interviews are rubric-scored, not unpredictable

College interviews are evaluated on a detailed rubric. Reading your own admissions file reveals what interviewers actually report on. The rubric has **12 categories across 3 parts**; captured from the source:

**Part 1 — Personal Qualities:**
1. How you present your personal traits
2. Your key characteristics
3. What kind of roommate you'd be
4. The impression you leave during conversations

(Parts 2–3 of the rubric were not fully captured in processed material; treat remaining categories as covering intellectual/academic engagement and school fit, and answer accordingly.) [DEALgiluHWn slides]

## Question playbooks (captured)

- **"Tell me about yourself"**: avoid the basic recitation of resume items; structure a narrative answer (her video covers this exact question) [Cph8_9Rtq7G]
- **"Greatest weakness?"**: real weakness + growth framing, not humble-brags [Cp5ExClsgBe]
- **School-specific questions ("Why NYU?")**: use past→present→future — concrete personal experience tied to the school → aligned academic direction → specific future engagement there. Generic praise = rejection-grade. If the school name is swappable, the answer fails. [DTwVRxGkr-e]
- **End-of-interview questions**: always ask something substantive at the end (she has a dedicated post on what to ask) [CrOC5B1LXqw]

## Preparation stance

She attributes her interview success to knowing the rubric in advance and answering "with purpose" rather than winging it. Anxiety before Ivy interviews is normal; cohesive on-the-spot answers are a trainable skill, not charisma. [DEALgiluHWn]

## Practical prep protocol (synthesized from corpus)

1. Draft 5–6 story modules from your theme that can flex into most questions
2. For each school: 2–3 specific, researched hooks (programs, professors, traditions) tied to your past
3. Practice aloud; check answers against the rubric categories above
4. Prepare 2–3 genuine questions for the interviewer
