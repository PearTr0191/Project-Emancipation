# UILG Voice & Style Profile

> **Read this BEFORE writing your own essays.** UILG is the most-followed U.S. college-admissions creator; her formula is the default the admissions committee has read 10,000 times. The point of this document is to make sure you can *recognize* her patterns so you can write **against** them with intent. Do not copy any of these patterns by accident. Do not use her hooks, her number-led carousel openers, or her house voice. You are building a different portfolio for a different reader.

> 2026-08-26 voice rule from `mentor/PS-theme-outline.md` §4 applies: "raw material must stay human-authored per 2026 AI policy." UILG's captions themselves are mostly her own voice, but the *system* of patterns (her hooks, her structures, her hashtag formula) is what AOs are tired of. Your job is to be the human reader's voice, not the algorithm's.

---

## 1. UILG's account at a glance

- **Name:** Elise, "Ultimate Ivy League Guide™"
- **Pitch:** "I graduated valedictorian and received ZERO Ivy League rejections. Now at Harvard. I'll guide you." Bio is a credibility stack: zero rejections → Harvard placement → featured in Forbes → millions of students guided → Ultimate Mentor program.
- **Voice stance:** Mentor / authority / older-sister-but-with-proof. Not academic. Not peer. Sells competence and access.
- **Product funnel:** Every post ends with a CTA — "Comment [KEYWORD] and I'll send you my [free guide / course / eBook]." The keyword is what triggers her DM automation. Captions are 30% value, 70% funnel.

## 2. Caption formulas (do not adopt)

### 2.1 The "How to X" hook

> "HOW TO ANSWER 'WHAT IS YOUR GREATEST WEAKNESS?' DURING AN INTERVIEW📝"
> "HOW TO BE THE SMARTEST PERSON IN YOUR CLASS📝"
> "HOW TO WRITE A DBQ📝"
> "HOW TO STUDY FOR A LONG TIME & NOT LOSE FOCUS📝"

**Pattern:** ALL-CAPS verb phrase, 📝 emoji, question mark or statement. The "📝" is her signature glyph — she uses it on 60–80% of educational posts. It functions as a *content-type signal* to her audience: "this is a study-tip post."

**Why it loses impact at scale:** AOs reading 30+ applications have seen the "How to write a college essay / be a better student / ace the SAT" caption on Instagram so many times that the words no longer register. They are training their eye to skip it.

**Your alternative:** open with a *scene*, a *question with a specific answer*, or a *single sharp claim*. Avoid second-person imperatives as a hook unless the second-person is doing narrative work (e.g., "You will read this and think it is about X; it is about Y").

### 2.2 The "7 X" list

> "7 free certifications for High School students!"
> "7 full ride scholarships to apply to!"
> "7 STEM competitions open for High School students!"
> "12 College App terms explained like you're 12!"
> "20 college essay hook examples!"

**Pattern:** Odd or round number + noun + "!" or implied surprise. She sometimes trades "7" for "10", "18", "25" — the count varies, the structure doesn't.

**Why it loses impact at scale:** "7 tips" is a content-creator tic, not a student tic. AOs have read "7 tips for [essay / school list / interview]" hundreds of times. The number also creates a countability illusion: a "7 tips" post with 3 weak tips and 4 recycled tips reads as filler.

**Your alternative:** If you must list, name the *quality*, not the count. "Three things my recommender actually wrote about me" beats "3 LOR tips." Better: don't list at all. Pick one and go deep.

### 2.3 The keyword-CTA close

> "Comment ACTIVITY for my full extracurricular guide! ✅"
> "Comment COACH for more help with your college applications! 🧠"
> "Comment MONEY for my full guide to financial aid & how to pay for college! 💰 ⭐️"
> "Comment SENIOR and I'll send you my senior year college admissions guide!"

**Pattern:** Capitalized keyword, exclamation, emoji. The keyword is the same on every post in a series (ACTIVITY → ACTIVITY guide; SENIOR → SENIOR guide). The keyword is also the DM trigger — she is *training* her followers to comment a single word.

**Why it loses impact at scale:** None. This is not for AOs; it's for her funnel. **Do not adopt this for essays.** For the Common App, no CTA exists. For supplements, the closest analogue is the closing line of the response — that is the ONE place you can land a small, controlled CTA: *show, don't ask*.

**Your alternative for PS close:** Land on a *beat* (a small sensory image, a single line that reframes the opening). Not "comment below." Not "follow for more." A PS is a closed object, not a funnel.

### 2.4 The credibility-on-loop

> "After receiving ZERO Ivy League rejections and now at the top 5% of my class at Harvard University, I realized that the college admission process is a level playing field…"

**Pattern:** Self-promo paragraph embedded mid-caption. "Zero rejections / Harvard / Forbes / valedictorian / millions of students / mentor program" — these phrases recur across months of posts.

**Why it loses impact at scale:** AOs are not the audience for her credibility loop. They're the audience for your *specific* voice. Her credibility loop is fine in the *pitch* (DM), irrelevant in the *artifact* (essay).

**Your alternative:** Don't loop. Don't pitch. Show one specific moment that lets the AO infer the rest.

### 2.5 The hash-tag stack

> "#UltimateIvyLeagueGuide #UltimateMentor #UILG"
> "#ultimateivyleagueguide #uilg #ultimatementor"

**Pattern:** 1 brand hashtag + 1-2 casing variants. The lowercase variants are for discoverability (the uppercase looks like display; the lowercase looks like organic). She keeps the total count to 3.

**Your takeaway:** Tag economy, not tag volume. Don't stack 30 hashtags. Don't use any on the essay. (Common App doesn't render them.) This is a meta-pattern — not useful for the essay itself, but useful as a reminder that *compression* reads as confident.

## 3. Structural templates (recognize, don't copy)

### 3.1 The carousel slide structure

UILG carousels (her highest-engagement format) follow a near-constant 7–9 slide template:

1. **Cover (slide 1)** — bold hook, single image, brand name at bottom. Sometimes numbered.
2. **Context (slide 2)** — "Most students think X. Here's the truth."
3. **List items (slides 3–8)** — one item per slide, often with a numbered or icon mark. Each item is 1–3 lines max.
4. **CTA (last slide)** — "Comment [KEYWORD]" + the follow-on benefit.

**Why it loses impact at scale:** It works for the platform (swipe-through = high engagement). It does NOT translate to a 650-word essay. AOs are not swiping. They are reading 50 essays in a sitting and the carousel-template's residue (bullet-density, "tip 1 / tip 2 / tip 3" structure) shows up in essays as the **list essay** failure mode.

**Your alternative:** Essay structure is scene-arc, not slide-arc. Open on a moment. Go deep on one. End on the same image with new light. See `mentor/PS-theme-outline.md` for the locked two-movement structure.

### 3.2 The reel speech pattern

UILG reels (her highest-volume format) follow a tight pattern:

- **Open (0–3 s):** Bold claim OR curiosity gap OR name-drop ("This one decision can either 3x your odds…")
- **Body (3–45 s):** Numbered points, fast cuts, text overlay matching the speech. Often 3 points, sometimes 5.
- **Close (45–60 s):** "Comment [KEYWORD] and I'll send you…" + brand watermark.

**Why it loses impact at scale:** The reel format *requires* punchy compression. A 60-second reel teaches the viewer to think in 20-second chunks. When a student writes in this voice, the essay feels *fast* in a way that works against it — AOs want the writer to slow down, to linger on a single moment, to trust the reader's attention.

**Your alternative:** Slow the pace. Stay in one scene for at least 80 words. Earn the cut.

### 3.3 The "I wish I knew" retrospective

> "If I could give advice to my 17 year old self, here's what I'd say 🎓📝"
> "If I had to redo 11th Grade, here's what I'd do to get into the Ivy League 🧠🎓"
> "What I believed in high school vs. the true reality…"

**Pattern:** "If I had to redo X" / "What I wish I knew in X" / "X vs reality." It's the "letter to my younger self" template — a staple of mentor content. The hook promises vulnerability; the body usually delivers tips.

**Why it loses impact at scale:** The "letter to younger self" frame is itself the cliché. AOs reading thousands of essays have seen "If I could go back in time" / "Looking back on my high school years" openers so often they've become invisible. They are also a *mentor* move, not a *student* move. They position the writer as the sage, not the subject.

**Your alternative:** Stay in the present. Stay in the scene. The PS is not advice. It is *experience* rendered.

## 4. Phrases to ban from your own writing

A small blacklist of UILG-loops and broader creator-content-loops that AOs are trained to skim past. None of these belong in your PS or supplements. Most of them are also banned in the no-slop skill, but worth calling out specifically because they are heavily *associated* with the UILG voice.

- "since I was a child"
- "ever since I was young"
- "my passion for X"
- "I learned the value of"
- "I learned that hard work pays off"

- "change the world"

## 5. What to take from UILG (the small useful part)

Most of UILG's content is *pitch* + *framework*. The frameworks are useful for **strategy**, not for **voice**. Things to keep in your strategic toolbox:

- **PPF (Past, Present, Future)** for supplemental essays where the school asks "Why this major?" — connect a concrete past moment → your current work → your future direction. (See `knowledge/common-app-essay.md`.)
- **Three Tiers** for activity portfolio architecture — one Tier 1 (your pillar), a few Tier 2 (supporting), a few Tier 3 (color). (See `knowledge/activities-list.md`.)
- **Six-step personal statement build** as a *checklist* (not a template) — used to verify the draft, not to write it. (See `knowledge/common-app-essay.md`.)
- **ED three-step rule** for any school you're considering for ED/REA — Common Data Set + net price calculator + ED trifecta. (See `knowledge/school-list-strategy.md`.)
- **"What's Better: X or Y?"** is a useful *internal* test for any decision (essay topic, ED choice, recommender choice) — but it should be answered with one sharp paragraph, not a carousel.

What NOT to take:

- Her hooks (the ALL-CAPS How-to-X, the 7-X lists, the keyword CTA closes).
- Her credibility loops (zero rejections / Harvard / millions of students).
- Her hashtag stack (irrelevant to essays).
- Her "letter to younger self" retrospective (cliché; AOs are tired of it).
- Her visual style (numbered slide markers, big emoji, brand watermark) — irrelevant to plain text.

## 6. How AOs read the UILG-shaped applicant

The danger is not that an AO *knows* UILG. The danger is that the AO has been trained, by seeing thousands of UILG-fed applicants, to *pattern-match* certain writing moves to "mentor-coached template." When they see those moves, they slow down looking for the authentic voice underneath. If there isn't one, the application is filed under "mentor-coached" and gets no bonus for the coaching.

Markers an AO has been trained to look for:

- Bullet-density in "essay" (multiple short paragraphs that read like slides).
- "I learned the value of…" / "this experience taught me…" closers.
- The 5-paragraph essay structure (intro / 3 body / conclusion) used as a cage.
- "Looking back on my high school years" / "If I could go back in time" opens.
- Excessive colon use ("Three things: resilience, curiosity, and empathy").
- Lists of adjectives ("hard-working, passionate, dedicated, and driven").
- Name-dropping the school in a way the AO has seen 500 times ("Since I was seven, I dreamed of attending [School]. As soon as I stepped on campus…").

If any of these are in your draft, cut. The draft is only ready when an AO can read the first 50 words and *not* know what mentor / creator / template you used.

---

## 7. One-line summary

**UILG's content is the noise your voice has to be heard over.** She is a very good operator of a very good template. The template is what the AO is sick of. The voice — yours — is what they're looking for. Don't import her template. Don't even import her anti-template tricks ("I'll write the OPPOSITE of UILG!") because that's still a derivative. Write the thing only you can write, slowly, in scenes, and let the AOs hear a person they haven't heard from yet.
