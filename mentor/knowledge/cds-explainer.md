# Common Data Set (CDS) — Explainer

## What it is

The Common Data Set is a standardized annual questionnaire that every U.S. college publishes. It's the same form every school fills out, which is the whole point: you can compare institutions on a common template. It is maintained collaboratively by the College Board, Peterson's, and U.S. News.

**What it is NOT:** admissions decisions, or a crystal ball. It is the institution's own self-reported data from the prior cycle. It tells you what the school *says* it values and what its enrolled class *looked like* — not what your odds are.

## The sections that matter for you

### Section C7 — the weighting grid
This is the highest-leverage section. Every school rates each factor on a four-level scale:
- **Very Important**
- **Important**
- **Considered**
- **Not Considered**

Factors include: rigor of secondary school record, class rank, academic GPA, standardized test scores, application essay, recommendations, interview, extracurricular activities, talent/ability, character/personal qualities, first-generation status, alumni relation, geographical residence, state residency, religious affiliation, volunteer work, work experience, and level of applicant's interest.

**What it tells you:** where the school actually puts its energy. Marketing says one thing; C7 says another. Use it to decide where to invest your effort and which schools fit your profile.

### Sections C9–C12 — the score table
SAT/ACT 25th, 50th, and 75th percentile scores for enrolled students who submitted. Also the share of enrolled students who submitted each test.

**What it tells you:** where your score sits relative to the middle of the admitted class. At or above the 50th percentile = you're in the pool. Below the 25th = real headwind. Note the 75th percentile, not the median — at top schools the 75th is often a perfect 800 Math.

### Section C8 — test policy
Whether the school requires, recommends, or ignores SAT/ACT for Fall 2027 entry. Superscore policy is sometimes here too.

### Section C1 — enrollment, majors, retention
Useful for "why us" essays — which programs exist, how big the school is.

## How to use it (the workflow)

1. Pull each school's CDS (search "[school] Common Data Set 2025-2026").
2. Read C7. If extracurriculars are "Very Important" and your Tier 1 asset is a shipped product, that school is a better fit than one that rates them merely "Considered."
3. Read C9-C12. Check your SAT composite (and Math specifically if you're STEM) against the 50th percentile.
4. Cross-check C8. If the school requires scores and you're below its 25th percentile, that's a reach — not a target.

## Honest limits

- **Self-reported.** A school's CDS is its own numbers, audited lightly. Admit rates can lag and are rounded.
- **No odds.** Percentiles tell you the shape of the admitted class, not your probability. Two applicants at the same score get different decisions.
- **Doesn't capture qualitative fit.** Essay voice, whether the class is already full of your major, and reader mood aren't in any section.
- **C7 is a snapshot.** A school's stated priorities shift year to year. Treat it as current-cycle signal, not eternal truth.

## 2026-cycle watch

- **Stanford** reinstated its testing requirement for Fall 2026 entry (Class of 2030), and moved standardized test scores back to "Very Important" in C7.
- **Dartmouth** was the first Ivy to reinstate the requirement (Class of 2029 onward); scores have been rated "Very Important" throughout, including the test-optional years when they were submitted.
- Several other Ivies and top tiers have followed. If a school on your list still says "test-optional," re-check its CDS each cycle — the policy is in flux.
