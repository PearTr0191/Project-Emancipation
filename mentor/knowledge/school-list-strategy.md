## CDS comparison: Stanford vs Dartmouth (2025–26 cycle)

Source: Stanford CDS 2025–26, Dartmouth CDS 2025–26, as reported by College Transitions, Collegedata.fyi, and the institutions' own PDFs. For the CDS definition and section map, see `knowledge/cds-explainer.md`.

### Section C7 — how each school weights factors

| Factor | Stanford | Dartmouth |
|---|---|---|
| Rigor of secondary school record | Very Important | Very Important |
| Class rank | Very Important | Very Important |
| Academic GPA | Very Important | Very Important |
| Standardized test scores | Very Important | Very Important |
| Application essay | Very Important | Very Important |
| Recommendations | Very Important | Very Important |
| Extracurricular activities | Very Important | Very Important |
| Talent / ability | Very Important | **Important** (the one tier-down) |
| Character / personal qualities | Very Important | Very Important |
| Interview | Considered | Considered |
| First-generation status | Considered | Considered |
| Alumni/ae relation | Considered | Considered |
| Geographic residence | Considered | Considered |
| Volunteer work | Considered | Considered |
| Work experience | Considered | Considered |
| State residency | Not Considered | Not Considered |
| Religious affiliation | Not Considered | Not Considered |
| Level of applicant's interest | Not Considered | Not Considered |

**Reading the grid:**

- **Stanford has no "Important" tier.** Nine factors are Very Important and everything else drops to Considered. It treats the academic core, the essay, recommendations, extracurriculars, talent, and character as equally weighted. That means academic excellence clears the baseline but does not decide the outcome — the file has to convey a distinctive strength in the non-academic columns too.
- **Dartmouth has exactly one "Important" factor: talent/ability.** Everything else it cares about is Very Important. That's the single structural difference between the two. For an applicant whose Tier 1 asset is a *built product* rather than a competition award or an art/athletic talent, this matters: Stanford rates raw talent/ability at the top tier, Dartmouth rates it one step below extracurricular depth and character.

**What this means for your profile (ViDrive is your Tier 1):**
- At **Stanford**, extracurriculars, talent, and character are all Very Important — a shipped, quantified product lands squarely in the columns Stanford reads hardest. Depth over breadth is explicitly what they reward.
- At **Dartmouth**, your extracurricular depth and character are Very Important, but raw "talent/ability" is demoted to Important. If your strongest claim is *doing* (building, shipping) rather than *being* a recognized prodigy, Dartmouth's grid is marginally less aligned with your asset type than Stanford's.

This is a small signal, not a decision. Both schools rate extracurriculars at the top; both require scores; both are reaches.

### Section C9–C12 — the score table

| SAT submit rate | ~56% | ~69% |

**Where you land:** your 1540 (800 Math / 760 Verbal) sits at Stanford's *below-median* SAT composite and slightly above Dartmouth's median. On Math specifically you're at Stanford's 75th percentile (800) and well above Dartmouth's 75th (790). On EBRW you're at Stanford's median (760) and above Dartmouth's median (750).

Net: your score is *in range at both*, marginally stronger relative to Dartmouth on composite, and strongest of all on the Math column at Stanford — which is the column that matters most for a Data Science/AI applicant.

### Section C8 — test policy

- **Stanford:** SAT/ACT **required** for Fall 2026 entry (Class of 2030). Reinstated after a faculty committee found scores predict Stanford performance. Superscores both SAT and ACT.
- **Dartmouth:** SAT/ACT **required**. First Ivy to reinstate (Class of 2029). Superscores both. Scores due by Feb 1 for fall admission.

Both are test-mandatory. Your 1540 clears the bar at both; neither school has a published minimum.

### Honest caveats

- **Dartmouth's nonacademic C7 columns came through garbled** in the raw PDF extraction (it rendered every factor as "Very Important," which is not credible). The table above uses College Transitions' careful reading of the same documents. The one substantive claim — talent/ability as the single "Important" factor — is corroborated across multiple secondary sources; treat the exact Considered/Not Considered rows for the lower-tier Dartmouth factors as lower-confidence.
- **These are self-reported snapshots** from the prior cycle. Neither is your odds.
- **Neither tells you about fit.** C7/C9-C12 say what the school weighs; it doesn't say whether you'd thrive there. That's the supplementals' job.

### Practical takeaway

If you had to differentiate the two on CDS evidence alone: Stanford's grid is slightly more aligned with a builder profile (talent/ability at top tier), and your Math score is a stronger asset there. Dartmouth's grid is nearly identical but demotes raw talent one notch, and its admitted composite range sits ~30 points lower — meaning your score is less of a liability and more of an asset there. Both remain reaches; neither is winnable on CDS data alone.
