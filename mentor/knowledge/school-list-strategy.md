Source: Stanford CDS 2025–26, Dartmouth CDS 2025–26, as reported by College Transitions, Collegedata.fyi, and the institutions' own PDFs. For the CDS definition and section map, see `knowledge/cds-explainer.md`.

**Reading the grid:**

- **Stanford has no "Important" tier.** Nine factors are Very Important and everything else drops to Considered. It treats the academic core, the essay, recommendations, extracurriculars, talent, and character as equally weighted. That means academic excellence clears the baseline but does not decide the outcome — the file has to convey a distinctive strength in the non-academic columns too.
- **Dartmouth has exactly one "Important" factor: talent/ability.** Everything else it cares about is Very Important. That's the single structural difference between the two. For an applicant whose Tier 1 asset is a *built product* rather than a competition award or an art/athletic talent, this matters: Stanford rates raw talent/ability at the top tier, Dartmouth rates it one step below extracurricular depth and character.

**What this means for your profile (ViDrive is your Tier 1):**
- At **Stanford**, extracurriculars, talent, and character are all Very Important — a shipped, quantified product lands squarely in the columns Stanford reads hardest. Depth over breadth is explicitly what they reward.
- At **Dartmouth**, your extracurricular depth and character are Very Important, but raw "talent/ability" is demoted to Important. If your strongest claim is *doing* (building, shipping) rather than *being* a recognized prodigy, Dartmouth's grid is marginally less aligned with your asset type than Stanford's.

This is a small signal, not a decision. Both schools rate extracurriculars at the top; both require scores; both are reaches.

The table above uses College Transitions' careful reading of the same documents. The one substantive claim — talent/ability as the single "Important" factor — is corroborated across multiple secondary sources; treat the exact Considered/Not Considered rows for the lower-tier Dartmouth factors as lower-confidence.
- **These are self-reported snapshots** from the prior cycle. Neither is your odds.
- **Neither tells you about fit.** C7/C9-C12 say what the school weighs; it doesn't say whether you'd thrive there. That's the supplementals' job.

### Practical takeaway

If you had to differentiate the two on CDS evidence alone: Stanford's grid is slightly more aligned with a builder profile (talent/ability at top tier), and your Math score is a stronger asset there. Dartmouth's grid is nearly identical but demotes raw talent one notch, and its admitted composite range sits ~30 points lower — meaning your score is less of a liability and more of an asset there. Both remain reaches; neither is winnable on CDS data alone.
