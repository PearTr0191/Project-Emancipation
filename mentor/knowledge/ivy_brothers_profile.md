# Ivy Brothers Voice & Style Profile

> **Read this BEFORE writing your own essays.** Ivy Brothers is an admissions CONSULTING FIRM ("Admissions firm for those who refuse to settle. For families that play to win") — not a mentor persona. Its content is the fear-register version of admissions content: everything is a trap, a backdoor, or something schools "won't say." The point of this document is recognition, not adoption. Do not import the fear register, the firm voice, or the game frame into any essay. (Companion to `uilg-voice-profile.md` — read both.)

## 1. The account at a glance

- **Name:** Ivy Brothers (@ivybrothersofficial), theivybrothers.com — 668 posts, ~45K followers.
- **Pitch:** an agency selling former-admission-officer access — "1000+ Ivy League Acceptances," "100+ Counselors & Admission officers," "AI Research Specialist" watermark, "get into any college in 3 weeks" / "21 days" claims.
- **Voice stance:** operator/firm — sells access and edge. Fear-based framing ("cooked," "average Joes," "families that play to win") creates urgency for the consult funnel.
- **Content format:** text-on-image carousels — the school-specific series ("What X Is Really Looking For (But Won't Say)" / "backdoor" series) is the core unique asset; reels carry the funnel ("Comment LEGACY to get DM'd the full interview clip").
- **Funnel:** a harder CTA than UILG's comment-keyword — consult slots ("We have former admission officers taking calls through Ivy Brothers right now... limited slots left"). The 7-levels taxonomy ("Which level are you?") is the funnel's self-positioning: level 2 = "the enlightened ones" = their students; level 1 = "admissions overlords" using "back doors and side doors."

## 2. What the account does better than anyone in the corpus

- **The school-by-school AO-priority series:** supplement prompts mapped one-to-one onto published institutional criteria (Notre Dame's three criteria → three short answers; JHU's bridge-builder prompt = its SNF Agora agenda turned into a question; Brown's Open Curriculum as exam; Columbia's Core as the tell). The corpus's best tactical intel — captured in `knowledge/ivybrothers-school-intel.md`.
- **Aid mechanics with asterisks:** the need-blind taxonomy (6 truly need-blind schools for every applicant on Earth), the Dean's Interest List mechanics from the SFFA v. Harvard trial record, the fee-waiver scrutiny pattern.
- **The deferral taxonomy and the ED-card map** — the corpus's only school-by-school treatment of how each school uses ED.

## 3. What NOT to take (the fear register + the firm voice)

- **The fear register:** "cooked," "average Joes," "the game," "families that play to win." AOs read none of this; it's funnel pressure. An essay written in this voice reads as transactional.
- **The game frame:** "admissions is gameable" — the system-as-exploit stance. The student's essays must NOT read as gamed; `The 5% Gap` theme already carries the honest version (the gap is real, the system is detectable, the voice stays human).
- **The firm's sell lines** ("get into any college in 3 weeks") — unfalsifiable marketing claims, not strategy.
- **The number-led hooks and the 10-type/5-myth list structure** — the same template disease as UILG (see `uilg-voice-profile.md` §2).

**Both are the noise your voice has to be heard over.** UILG trains AOs to skim the aspirational template; Ivy Brothers trains them to skim the fear/insider template. The applicant who wins is the one whose first 50 words sound like neither.

## 5. One-line summary

**Ivy Brothers is the fear-register version of the same template.** Take its tactical intel (school-specific supplements, aid asterisks, ED mechanics — all captured in the KB). Leave its voice: no fear register, no game frame, no firm speak. Write like the person the template can't produce.
