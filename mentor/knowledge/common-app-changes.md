# Common App Changes & Policy Watch

> Regulation-sensitive content. Verify current status before acting — policies shift cycle to cycle.

## Common App 2025 updates (confirmed in source speech, June 2025 post)

Two structural changes announced by Common App:

1. **Additional Information section: 650 → 300 words.** Half the space means ruthless prioritization — only context that changes how an AO reads the application.
2. **"Community Disruption" section renamed "Challenges and Circumstances."** Broader framing; same function of explaining disrupted schooling.

### The 4-step protocol for every item you list there
1. Say what happened, how it affected you, and what you did about it
2. Use bullet points, avoid fancy language
3. Think like an AO: what context do they need to *fairly* understand my application?
4. Do NOT paste supplemental essays or repeat anything already elsewhere in the application [DLU38sUxwF5]

## Post-submission additions — the mechanism (verified 2026-10-02)

**The Common App itself is locked.** Common App's applicant-support position: the application "is designed to be completed one time and submitted to all of the colleges at once, or over a period of time. If you need to change information on a submitted application, you must contact the college's admissions office directly to ask how they would like you to proceed." Counselor guides corroborate the mechanics: post-submission edits "will only be included in new submissions." So there is **no global add button** on the Common App, and no single edit propagates to the schools you already submitted to. *Sourcing note: the Common App support article is Salesforce-hosted and returned a CSS error on direct fetch; the text above is from the search-indexed excerpt of that article on `appsupport.commonapp.org`, corroborated by two independent counselor guides (Collegewise 2025-26, Liaison transfer help center). The per-school channels below were read directly off each school's own admissions page and are firmer than the Common App position.*

**What each school does instead — verified channels:**

| School | Channel | Verified language | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Stanford** (first-year) | Stanford portal → **Update Application Form**, in the "Updates and Questions" section | "To add new information, such as an award, to your submitted application, please log in to your Stanford portal and complete the Update Application Form." Same form for corrections. | `admission.stanford.edu/apply/first-year/submitted.html`, updated Sept 11, 2026 |
| **Dartmouth** | Applicant portal upload (fastest), or `submit@dartmouth.edu`, or fax | Supplemental Materials page lists "**Updates regarding:** awards received, championships won, participation in an event, newspaper articles that feature you." Portal upload is "the fastest means by which to add new information." Email/fax allow 10 business days to be recorded. | `admissions.dartmouth.edu/glossary-term/supplemental-materials`; waitlist page confirms the same upload route |

**Stanford's limit, worth knowing:** Stanford's *transfer* page states "We do not add supplementary materials like writing samples, research papers, or other special work to our application file, and we do not offer an option for applicants to upload supplementary materials." The first-year page does not repeat this, so **do not assume** it applies to first-year — but plan on the Update Application Form text box, not an upload, and never send a paper. Stanford also warns that links inside the form get no guarantee of review.

| **NYU** (first-year) | Applicant portal → **"Personal Information Update Request"**; fallback email `admissions@nyu.edu` | Update form is documented and covers test scores and essays — but the **published list of updatable fields does not include awards or honors**. NYU also states: "We cannot consider other supplemental materials like research papers or photos." | `nyu.edu/admissions/undergraduate-admissions/how-to-apply.html` |

**The pattern is not uniform, which is the point.** Stanford and Dartmouth name awards explicitly as accepted updates. NYU has a documented update channel but awards are absent from its published field list — so an award there is an email, not a form. Treat every school's channel as needing its own check; do not assume a Stanford-shaped route exists elsewhere. This student's RD slate is Stanford, NYU, Northeastern, Georgia Tech, USC, Michigan, CMU, Purdue, Maryland, Illinois Tech (Michigan/GT/Purdue moved to RD when the public EAs were dropped on aid grounds).

**January checklist for a late result**, one line per school, done in the week the result lands:
1. Search the school's admissions site for "update application" / "additional information" / "supplemental materials."
2. If a form exists, use it. If the field list doesn't cover awards, email the admissions address published on the same page.
3. Say what the award is, its date, and its level (school / regional / national) in two sentences. No essay, no elaboration.
4. One action per school. Don't attach certificates or images unless asked — Stanford, NYU and Dartmouth all restrict or discourage uploads.
5. Skip any school whose decision already released. An upload after a decision does nothing.

**No pre-alert channel for competition results.** The Common App's "number of future SAT/ACT sittings you expect" field does pre-alert a school that more information is coming — but it is scoped to test scores only, and this student correctly plans 0 (no retake). There is no equivalent field for a late competition result. An award you learn about in January is a January upload, full stop.

**Standing rule for this file:** a post-submission addition is a *maintenance* action, not a *strategy*. The AO read is minutes long and committee review runs on its own calendar, so a January upload lands after the first read at most schools. Cheap, worth doing, never reorganize the application around it — and verify each school's channel from its own admissions page, because acceptance of late updates is at each college's discretion.

## Supplement diffs captured from the Ivy Brothers crawl (2026-09-21)

- **Columbia 2026-27 supplement quietly tripled** — most guides still describe the old one. Now six pieces: one 100-word comma-separated list (no explanations allowed) + five 150-word essays (unique perspective / engaging a disagreeing viewpoint / navigating adversity / why Columbia / specific academic interest). Applicants working from old guides are planning for a shorter, easier supplement than the one they will face. [DctksHBgCyE]
- **Notre Dame supplement got shorter** — 3 of 5 short answers → 2 of 4 + the 150-word essay; compliment prompt cut, "what would you fight for" kept. [DdXRg64CcDP]
- **Notre Dame added a video portion** (last November) — the essays are no longer the only place they hear the applicant's voice. Watch whether other top schools follow; a video ask changes prep time. [DdXRg64CcDP]
- **Test-optional end dates (as captured):** Columbia — required again starting 2027-28; Notre Dame — optional holds this cycle, ends next year; Hopkins — required for fall 2026 entry; Brown — mandatory since the Class of 2029. [DctksHBgCyE, DdXRg64CcDP, DdhJwCZCSt4, Dc_S2DFCW__]
- **USC added binding Early Decision** (Feb 2026, Fall 2027 applicants) — a new round at a major non-Ivy; mechanics in `ivybrothers-school-intel.md`. [DcjGGRFFnRl]
- The cross-school rule is reaffirmed with new evidence: re-read a school's CURRENT supplement prompts each cycle as the signal of what they now weight — **the prompts are the agenda** (Hopkins' bridge-builder prompt = its SNF Agora dialogue institute turned into a question). [DctksHBgCyE, DdhJwCZCSt4]
