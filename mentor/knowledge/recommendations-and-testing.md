# Recommendations, Testing, Study Skills, International & Policy Notes

## Recommendation letters

- Junior-year teachers will likely be your recommenders → build genuine relationships during junior year so strong letters are easy to write [DIBoicsMYY3]
- Identify recommenders by summer before senior year: **one academic + one personal** recommender who truly know you and can vouch for your strengths [C82DnKrM_Lm]
- Letters must corroborate your theme — if your theme is "leadership through service," the letter should mention it; mismatched letters are a coherence red flag [DLm6QZ5h1UJ, DJ_zrMtsMZM]
- **One-pager protocol (2026)**: don't just hand over a resume — counselors handle hundreds of students. Bring a one-pager covering academic interests, personal challenges, and how you compare to peers, then ask: *"Here are three things I want my application to say. Could you weave one moment for each into your letter?"* You must supply the language. [DcbHs7gSf-U]
- Prep your recommenders: hand them a brief (your theme, moments, what you're arguing); one case study credits "prepping teachers to write standout letters that proved upward momentum" as decisive for an international applicant's Johns Hopkins admit [DK3FjmGShqM]

## Grades & standardized testing (positioning)

- High grades/SAT do NOT differentiate at top schools — everyone in the pool has them. Differentiators: future-success potential + application strategy. A 1600/4.0 "gets you in the door... but won't win you a spot." [DCzLzGMv6Kc, DcOPrIxB0wg]
- Mindset, potential, and reflection carry more weight than a perfect GPA. Efficient ≠ effective use of time. [C_IyP02uTTV]
- **Digital SAT strategy (2026)**: aim above the 50th percentile of admitted students at your target schools; budget 15 h foundation/assessment → 50 h intensive section practice → ≥3 full-length adaptive tests. The DSAT is adaptive: secure every easy question or the algorithm caps your score ceiling. [DYptnbDxQCM]
- Test scores interact with narrative: one case study emphasizes admits "despite not having strong enough test scores" when essays stood out [DSvcr3QAUjc slides]

## International students

- Recurring claim: international applicants lose spots by misunderstanding how different US admissions is — wasting time on things that don't matter while missing what does. Her listed failure modes: theme building, financial aid navigation, essay positioning in a global pool. [DLC3HBqMQpC]

## Testing era end + cross-school signals (Ivy Brothers crawl, 2026-09-21)

- **Test-optional is ending on this student's cycle:** Columbia was the last Ivy to stay test optional — required again starting 2027-28 (a multi-year faculty review found scores useful alongside grades and rigor). Notre Dame: optional holds for this cycle and ends next year. The series' consistent claim: every top college eventually returns to requiring scores, because a transcript alone cannot separate tens of thousands of nearly identical applicants. [DctksHBgCyE, DdXRg64CcDP]
- **"Optional" describes the policy, not the pool.** Submit if at/above the school's enrolled middle-50 band. Per-school bands captured in `ivybrothers-school-intel.md` (Hopkins 1530–1560 SAT / 34–36 ACT; Brown admits 1480–1560 / 34–35; Columbia submitters 1510–1560 / 34–36; Northwestern admits 1500–1570 / 34–35; Michigan 1360–1530; Notre Dame submitters 1460–1540; JHU — ~65% of a recent class carried a 4.0 unweighted). [DctksHBgCyE, Dc_S2DFCW__, DdZKNKICVc1, DcJX_nblo3Q, DdhJwCZCSt4, DdXRg64CcDP]
- **Self-selection effect, quantified (Brown):** when mandatory testing returned (Class of 2029), the ED pool fell 6,251 → 5,048 in one cycle — students without a competitive score self-selected out before admissions had to say no; the admit rate rose because the weak pool disappeared, not because Brown got easier. Practical read: a required-test school's post-reinstate rate is not comparable to its optional-era rate. [Dc_S2DFCW__]
- *This student: 1540 SAT (800 M / 760 V super-scored input) sits at/above every band above — submit everywhere scores are read; the 1540-stands advice holds and is now band-verified.*

## Recommendation-letter signals from the same crawl

- **Warmth and conviction beat a rehearsed brag sheet** (Georgetown: take the interview seriously and be a real person in it). The same standard applies to letters: the letter should sound like a person who knows you, not a template that contains you. [DbWG1NWifVS]
- **Duke scores recommendations as one of six categories** (10 points of 60 in Trinity, alongside curriculum, grades, testing, essays, extracurriculars/character) — a single weak area sinks otherwise strong applicants there. At point-system schools the letter is not garnish; it is 1/6 of the score. [DadlabACXhj]
- **Where letters carry extra weight:** Brown (no interview, no demonstrated-interest tracking — the essays and letters carry more weight than almost anywhere else); Michigan (interview not considered, no alumni network — the supplements are where interest registers; the letter corroborates). [Dc_S2DFCW__, DcJX_nblo3Q]
- **The counselor-rec one-pager script holds** (already captured above): supply the language — "here are three things I want my application to say; could you weave one moment for each into your letter?" [DcbHs7gSf-U]
