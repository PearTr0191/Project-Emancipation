# School-by-School AO-Priority Intel (Ivy Brothers crawl)

> Source: @ivybrothersofficial slide series ("What X Is Really Looking For (But Won't Say)" / "backdoor" series), extracted 2026-09-21 from the account's 2024–2026 post pool (343 feed posts; slide text via OCR). Numbers are as stated on the slides and attribute to the schools' own releases, Common Data Sets, or — for Harvard — the SFFA v. Harvard trial record. Treat every number as the creator's claim; verify against the school's own pages before acting. Student-relevance flags reference the locked list (Dartmouth ED, Stanford RD, NYU RD fallback; $7.5k/yr budget; Vietnamese international).

## Cross-school rules (the series' spine)

1. **You are read against your SCHOOL's pool, not the university.** UPenn (4 undergrad schools), Northwestern (6 schools), USC (by program), Michigan (one school inside Michigan): the pick sets the bar. Pick the one your file already argues for. [DdPE50uiXaJ, DdZKNKICVc1, DcjGGRFFnRl, DcJX_nblo3Q]
2. **Every school's supplement is a test of that school's defining feature** — answer the feature, not the school generally. Vague praise of the feature is the most common way a strong file loses. Per-school mapping below. [Dc_S2DFCW__, DctksHBgCyE, DdhJwCZCSt4, DdZKNKICVc1]
3. **ED is the single biggest lever almost everywhere** (RD runs 2–4× lower), and the series consistently advises: clear first choice + file ready by Nov 1 → ED. But the bump usually dies on deferral (Dartmouth deferred → ~5–10% RD; Northwestern ED denial = out for the year). [DagOuYNidaX, DdZKNKICVc1, DdPE50uiXaJ, DctksHBgCyE, DadlabACXhj, DcjGGRFFnRl]
4. **"Optional" describes the policy, not the pool.** Submit if at/above the school's enrolled middle-50 band. [DctksHBgCyE, DcJX_nblo3Q]
5. **One hyper-specific detail beats every adjective.** Name the actual place, text, lab, event. The reader who has read six thousand essays remembers the one that named something real. [DctksHBgCyE]
6. **The hooks that structurally move the needle:** URM, first-gen, recruited athlete, legacy, development case, faculty kid, QuestBridge. Everything else is contested on the file's merits. [DdPE50uiXaJ]

## Johns Hopkins [DdhJwCZCSt4]

- **Supplement = one 350-word "bridge builders" essay** (200-word floor enforced — a shorter answer will not submit). It is a viewpoint test, not a diversity essay. The most-submitted answer is a political disagreement — that is the largest pile. Differences of age, class, language, or money sit fresher because almost nobody writes them.
- The prompt is institutional: Hopkins built the SNF Agora Institute (2017) around dialogue across political difference, runs a Democracy Day, and has partnered with AEI and Heterodox Academy. A student who has genuinely stayed in a room with someone they disagreed with is answering it.
- **Peer positioning:** Vanderbilt = warm social leader; Rice = residential-college fit; Notre Dame/Georgetown = faith + service; WashU/Emory = polished all-rounder with a clean pre-med file — **at Hopkins that file is the most common strong rejection**. Closest comparison: Carnegie Mellon — both want evidence of work already underway, not potential. Hopkins has led federal research spending for 40+ years; the winning file is *already doing the work somewhere, at any scale, and can say plainly what the work was*.
- **ED:** Class of 2029 — 793 admitted of ~7,563 early applicants across ED I + ED II (~10.5%); RD ran ~4–5%; early fills ~60% of the class; two binding rounds open until January 2. **ED II is the most underused round at any top-ten school** (built for students denied/deferred in December). Waitlist took ~1.5% in recent years.
- **Legacy killed** (Daniels presidency): parents' degree does nothing; a line about a parent attending Hopkins is dead weight in the essay.
- **Money rules changed Nov 2025:** tuition-free under $200k family income; under $100k = tuition + fees + housing + living covered (zero parent contribution); need-blind + loan-free since the $1.8B Bloomberg gift (2018); Pell share 15.4% → 24.1% (2018→2025). Bigger pool next cycle — "the rate you are looking at now is the friendliest one you will see."
- **Testing required** for fall 2026 entry. Enrolled middle 50: 1530–1560 SAT / 34–36 ACT — level with Harvard and Caltech. ~65% of a recent class carried a 4.0 unweighted. The transcript gets you considered and nothing more.
- **Things that do nothing:** shadowing hours (every pre-med has them); "collaborative not cutthroat" (arrives thousands of times a year); treating the supplement as a why-school essay (naming professors without a reason); the political-disagreement answer again; being purely a science applicant (Hopkins runs Writing Seminars, Peabody, and a new School of Government and Policy — cross those lines).

## Northwestern [DdZKNKICVc1]

- **No more Why Northwestern essay** — replaced by one required 300-word question (background + how you'd engage with the community), plus five optional 200-word prompts: The Rock, a class/project you'd build, communities you'd join, the location, what your perspective adds in class. The personal statement is **not required** (they say you can be evaluated fully without it), and they tell you outright you may copy-paste from essays written for other colleges. Equal consideration for zero, one, or two optional answers.
- **Every prompt asks what you would do on campus with other people — not one asks what you have accomplished.** Northwestern scores whether you will show up and join things, not your intellect. Name a specific group, class, event, and your role in it; vague enthusiasm scores nothing.
- **The Rock question is a test most applicants miss:** guarding The Rock takes 24 hours, usually in shifts with a student group — the point was never the painting; it's a group of people sitting outside all night together. The stronger answer names who you'd be out there with and why.
- **Peer positioning:** UChicago tests strange thinking (puzzles); Northwestern's prompts are invitations. Williams/LACs want the student best in a room of twelve; **Northwestern wants the student who organizes the room**. The Ivies reward individual distinction; Northwestern rewards the joiner. Only private Big Ten — football game + 3.9 at the same time is the actual profile. **The file that loses most often: the brilliant loner with one deep obsession and no group around him.**
- **Six schools, the choice sticks:** Weinberg, McCormick, Medill, Communication, SESP, Bienen — you are read inside that pool. Medill/Communication want a specific kind of student; an undecided generalist looks lost. Bienen requires prescreening + audition on a timeline starting before November.
- **Quarter system is a personality filter:** three 10-week quarters, ~12–15 courses/year, finals three times a year. The student who does well carries four things at once and switches fast. If the whole application is one project sustained four years, Chicago and Williams will like it more.
- **Rates:** Class of 2029 ~7.5% overall, ED ~20%, RD ~5.3%. Class of 2030: >half the class admitted Dec 16, no rates published — **Northwestern no longer publishes official admission statistics**; every quoted number comes from a counselor letter or reporter.
- **Aid:** need-aware for internationals, small aid pool — asking for aid as an international lowers odds. Domestic: loan-free, meets full need. Test optional; among admits who submitted, middle 50 ~1500–1570 SAT / 34–35 ACT. ED denial = out for the year (no RD fallback). Interviews optional, alumni-run, neutral either way.

## Brown [Dc_S2DFCW__]

- **Open Curriculum as exam:** 30 courses, one declared concentration, demonstrated writing fluency — no distribution requirements means the requirement moved inside you. Admissions must find applicants who explore anyway; readers are explicitly trained to look for self-directed evidence (independent projects, years of sustained reading, advanced coursework taken from interest rather than requirement). A resume of required activities reads as someone who needs structure.
- **The "Why Brown" essay is the actual admissions test.** Every reader works the file asking: how will this student use a curriculum with no requirements? Vague/website-paraphrased answer = the most common reason a strong file loses. Name a specific concentration, specific course, a specific way you'd build your own path — not the concept of freedom itself.
- **Strategic major switching is visible:** declaring a less competitive concentration than your profile points to gets raised in committee.
- **ED mechanics:** admit count barely moves across four straight cycles (879 / 898 / 906 / 890) — what moves is the pool underneath, which is why the rate jumped 13.0% → 17.9% in one year without admitting more. **Read the admit count before the rate: the rate is a symptom, the count is the policy.** One binding ED round, no ED II.
- **Testing back (Class of 2029):** ED pool fell 6,251 → 5,048 in one cycle — students without a competitive score self-selected out; the rate rose because the weak pool disappeared, not because Brown got easier. Admit middle 50 now 1480–1560 SAT / 34–35 ACT. Below the band → a test-optional peer school is a better use of the application than hoping Brown reads past it.
- **Attention map:** no interview, no demonstrated-interest tracking — none of it reaches the file. Legacy is considered but "a very, very small consideration" (~8% of a recent class); a 2024 internal review left legacy exactly as it was. With no interview and no interest tracking, **the essays carry more weight at Brown than almost anywhere else**.

## UPenn [DdPE50uiXaJ]

- **Four undergrad schools; the pick sets the bar.** You are read against that school's pool. Wharton admits ~3–4% on its own — the hardest undergrad business admit in the country. Picking the school your file already points at is the highest-leverage move here.
- **Voice positioning:** Yale wants the life of the mind; **UPenn wants the person who is going to go build something and win.** "I want a career in X" is a strength here, not something to hide. The AO reads for someone who already thinks like an operator — a plan, a target, how the world actually works. The vague intellectual with no direction looks lost even with perfect stats.
- **Wharton essays:** "wanting to work at a bank" is the most-read essay in the pile. Applying business to a specific thing (healthcare, a family business, a policy area, an industry already worked in) is what gets in — Wharton says outright they want people who apply business to a real problem, not people who want the job.
- **Coordinated duals** (M&T, Huntsman, LSM): a separate, smaller admit on top of a single-digit rate — a reach stacked on a reach, own essays, own committee. Apply only if the pairing is the actual point; doing it for the name shows.
- **The actual UPenn admit:** the kid who ran a real thing in high school and can tell you the margins. Warm and pre-professional at once; college as the plan, not four years to figure it out. Hooks that move the needle: URM, first-gen, recruited athlete, legacy, development case, faculty kid, QuestBridge (QuestBridge filled 112 seats in the Class of 2030 before ED was read).
- **ED is not a small edge, it is the game:** fills ~51% of the class through binding ED. Class of 2028 ED rate 14.2% vs RD ~4% (~3.5×). UPenn stopped publishing round data after the Class of 2026 — you commit against a number they will not show you. Clear first choice + file ready Nov 1 → ED; RD is the hardest door in the Ivy League.

## Harvard [DbTmhWFCamJ, DdX3UB4ib3t]

- **They admit the 45-year-old, not the 17-year-old** — Harvard picks the person it wants carrying the name in 30 years. That is why IMO gold medalists get cut and kids with fewer awards get in: being the most accomplished teenager does not say who you become; where you are headed does. The file is read for the adult, not the student.
- **People skills is the unhooked score you live or die on:** generosity, humor, humility, whether you make the people around you better — the one you cannot grind for.
- **Voice contrast:** MIT wants people obsessed with ideas; Harvard wants people who move ideas through other people. Princeton kids write "I became fascinated by"; Harvard kids write "I built, I organized, I convinced." The lone genius who cannot pull people in loses.
- **Judgment over effort:** everyone in the pile worked hard — "I spent 2,000 hours" loses to "I realized everyone was solving the wrong problem." They bet on judgment because judgment is what matters after you leave.
- **They often reject the finished product:** the kid who already looks done gives Harvard less to work with. They bet on the person whose next 10 years their network can change. Stop looking accomplished; start looking still on the way up.
- **The money mechanics (SFFA v. Harvard trial record):** the dean testified money factors in and defended it. Dean's Interest List + Director's List: donors' relatives admitted ~42.7% (Classes 2014–2019) vs ~5.5% outside; 192 students in the Class of 2019 (>10% of the class) were on one of the lists. The rating forecasts money still to come — a long steady giving record sits inside the system; a single large gift from a stranger does not. Gift scale that moves anything: ~$1.1M–$8.7M, a funded building, a named fellowship — a five/low-six-figure check is a donation, not a development case. Legacy alone: ~33.6% admit vs <6% overall; ~5× boost as legacy, >7× on the dean's list (Arcidiacono et al. 2022). ~43% of white admits across those classes were athletes/legacies/dean's-list/faculty kids (~3/4 would have been rejected without the status). The Z list: admission conditional on a gap year, skews legacy/donor-connected, never published — "if you are reading about it here, you are not in it."
- **Rates + opacity:** Class of 2029: 2,003 admitted of 47,893 (~4.2%), yield 83.6%, first class required to submit scores again. Harvard stopped releasing admissions data during the cycle — one annual release each fall. If not an athlete/legacy/faculty child/on a list, you are competing for a far smaller pool of seats than 4.2% suggests.

## Dartmouth [DagOuYNidaX]

- **Fit is the actual filter:** ~4,500 undergrads in rural Hanover; they build a class of people who actually want *that* community, not people who want "an Ivy." Generic framing gets cut — name the D-Plan, the discussion-based classes, the specific Dartmouth thing. ~1 in 5 admits mention discourse and dialogue in their applications; they are admitting students who would do well at Williams/Amherst/Swarthmore. **If the "Why Dartmouth" could be pasted into a Penn or Columbia essay, it's done.**
- **Best at one thing:** skeptical of the jack-of-all-trades; irreplaceability beats breadth.
- **Geography is a real lever:** joined STARS (rural/small-town network) on purpose; ~15% of admits rural. Affluent Northeast suburb or major CA metro = the most saturated pool. Small town / Deep South / Great Plains works for you — structural, not luck. **International is an obstacle, not a boost:** ~15% of the class, but the international admit rate sits well below the ~6% overall. *Flag for this student: this is the honest ceiling on the Dartmouth ED bet as a Vietnamese international — the ED lever still applies, but the base rate inside that round is lower than the headline 17%.*
- **ED = the biggest edge:** fills nearly half the class; ED ~17% vs RD ~3.6%. Deferred ED → RD, where only 5–10% get in — the early bump does not carry over.
- **Obscure programs nobody mentions:** AB/BE engineering path (BA first, then a fifth-year accredited BE through Thayer); Tuck Bridge (a Tuck-run business credential at a school with no undergrad business major); the D-Plan off-terms for research/internships/study abroad that semester schools cannot match. Name them in the essay if they actually fit.
- **Reads for intellectual and personal qualities** — what you add to a tiny community is the whole question.

## Columbia [DctksHBgCyE]

- **The supplement quietly tripled — most guides still describe the old one.** For 2026-27 it is six pieces: one 100-word comma-separated list (no explanations allowed) plus five 150-word essays: your unique perspective, a time you engaged a disagreeing viewpoint, how you navigated adversity, why Columbia, and your specific academic interest. 150 words each = zero space for throat-clearing; every essay opens on the actual answer. Applicants working from an old guide are planning for a shorter, easier supplement than the one they will face.
- **Test-optional era ends with this cycle:** Columbia was the last Ivy to stay test optional — required again starting 2027-28. The stated reason: a multi-year faculty review found scores useful when read alongside grades and rigor. This cycle (2026-27) is still officially optional — it is also the last one. Every top college eventually returns to requiring scores because a transcript alone cannot separate 60,000 nearly identical applicants.
- **Optional on paper, competitive in practice:** among enrolled students who submitted, middle 50% SAT 1510–1560 / ACT 34–36. If at/above the band, send it — a strong score is one of the few purely objective advantages you control.
- **ED:** ~14.65% (C2027) / 13.2% (C2028) vs RD ~2.8–4% — three to four times the general rate, per Columbia's own CDS; no ED breakdown published since. Binding means binding — it only helps if Columbia is genuinely the first choice, not the statistically convenient one.
- **One hyper-specific detail beats every adjective:** "a welcoming community" could be written by every applicant; name the actual place (their example: the corner table at the Hungarian Pastry Shop). Same move in the classroom: a specific text, lab, or student publication, not the category. The reader who has read six thousand essays remembers the one that named something real.
- **The Core Curriculum is the tell:** almost every guide names the same failure — generic praise of the Core with no specific text, seminar, or idea attached. Reference an actual text on the syllabus, not the concept of a shared curriculum. "I love that Columbia is in New York" is the single most common sentence Columbia's readers see.

## USC [DcjGGRFFnRl]

- **USC added Early Decision (Feb 2026, after a Marshall pilot):** binding ED for nearly every major starting with Fall 2027 applicants — apply Nov 1, decision in December, a full month before EA hears anything. Dance, Music, Dramatic Arts sit out; Viterbi and Cinematic Arts are in. Not admitted early = deferred to Regular, not denied. At peers, ED runs 2–3× RD.
- **November 1 is the real deadline; January 10 is for people who don't read.** Applying by Nov 1 is the only way into the merit pool for most majors: full tuition Trustee, half Presidential, quarter Deans. RD applicants are not considered — no form, no appeal, no late door. Scale: 40,000+ apply, ~1,000 get finalist interviews. ED keeps full merit eligibility, so binding costs nothing on money.
- **You are applying to a major, not USC:** Marshall, Viterbi, Cinematic Arts each run their own gate. Portfolio/audition majors: a separate December 1 deadline with faculty review. The short answers are graded as why-this-program, not why-LA — name the lab, the outlet, the case competition. Class of 2030: 10.4% overall on 79,290 applications; the named programs run tighter.
- **AB 1780 (Sept 1, 2025): California bans legacy and donor preference at private colleges.** Last pre-ban class ~14.5% legacy. USC's line since: legacy is not a criterion but "remains a consideration"; compliance reports go to the state every June. Practical read: the formal boost is gone on paper; the fit case a Trojan family can write is not.
- **Rejection comes with fine print:** Spring admit (January enrollment, same degree/campus). Trojan Transfer Plan: invite-only, arrives at the bottom of select denial letters — a year of strong full-time grades elsewhere, and USC "typically finds a space" for students who hit the conditions. The transfer round has historically admitted well above the first-year rate.
- **The family playbook:** clear #1 + aid not deciding → ED. Merit money matters → EA by Nov 1 (RD forfeits Trustee and Presidential entirely). Arts majors: December 1 covers both admission and scholarship review. Spring admit / TTP = yes with terms, not a no.

## Michigan [DcJX_nblo3Q]

- **In-state is the real quota:** in-state admitted at roughly twice the out-of-state rate, and the class is built to keep it that way. The out-of-state pool is Ivy overflow (Northeast suburbs, Chicago, California) — the most saturated file stacks in the country. Out-of-state, treat Michigan like an Ivy: same rigor bar, same essay effort, none of the hometown cushion.
- **The transcript is the whole gate:** only two factors rate Very Important in Michigan's own reporting — course rigor and GPA. Everything else breaks ties among students who already cleared the bar. Educational environment is a scored category: you are graded against what your school offers, not against Exeter. Test optional on paper, but enrolled middle 50 = 1360–1530 — at/above that, submit. Legacy not considered.
- **The essays are the interview:** the interview is not considered; no alumni interview network (only Music/Theatre/Dance and some Engineering ever sit you down). Applicant interest is merely considered — with no interview to show it in, the supplements are where it registers. You are admitted to one school inside Michigan; if a sentence works with another university's name in it, it is filler.
- **Michigan added binding ED last cycle — a yield play:** mostly to lock committed out-of-state families at ~$86k all-in. ED and EA share the Nov 1 deadline; ED answers by Dec 24 and commits Jan 6. The strongest signal an out-of-state applicant controls. Postponed out of ED and the binding agreement dies — you re-enter the regular pool as a free agent.
- **The postpone is a test most families fail:** Michigan postpones early applicants in bulk instead of denying. A postpone means the file is alive and they want more evidence. Postponed students can add a brand-new SAT/ACT + first-term senior grades until Feb 1 — Michigan invites this in writing, and almost nobody does it. Formal channel: the Expression of Continued Interest form in Enrollment Connect, due March 1. **One strong update beats five small ones.**
- **Quiet doors this cycle:** the CS advance-selection gate is gone (open entry, while peer flagships still gate it); IBEM non-selects can now fall back into Ross BBA or Engineering instead of an outright denial; the Ross preferred-admission path through Engineering is sunset; Public Health joins as a new preferred-admission option through LSA.

## Duke [DadlabACXhj]

- **School spirit is real here:** Duke wants students who plug into the culture — the games, tenting, the whole social identity. Most top schools do not care if you are excited to be there; Duke selects against the cold, cerebral type. High horsepower wrapped in someone fun and collaborative wins. Pre-professional ambition is welcome (closer to Penn than to Brown or Yale — saying you want consulting, finance, or medicine is not a liability). They reward the connector: Trinity–Pratt link means policy+data, engineering+health over single-lane purists.
- **Duke scores on a point system:** two ratings (academic + personal), six categories worth 10 points each — 60 total in Trinity: curriculum, grades, testing, essays, recommendations, extracurriculars/character. Generally need above ~45/60 in Trinity for serious contention. More mechanical than people think — a single weak area sinks otherwise strong applicants; the system averages you out.
- **Client case (their example):** 3.6 GPA, test-optional (which they almost never recommend), one clear deep repeated spike carried the file — accessibility, via a founded organization matching low-income founders to grant/loan programs, with Forbes recognition. The lesson: one clear, deep, repeated spike beat a middling GPA and a missing test score.
- **Trinity vs Pratt is the strategic call:** you apply to one school; they read on different scales. Pratt skews toward numbers (engineering faculty on the committee; high test scores, real physics/math, things you built) — rewards high numbers with a thinner story. Trinity rewards a strong story even with slightly lower numbers. Elite stats but no narrative → Pratt is the play; narrative but softer numbers → Trinity.
- **ED fills ~half the class:** ED ~13–14% vs RD ~3.7% — the biggest lever a normal applicant has; a true ED-heavy school.
- **Obscure programs that signal real fit:** Program II (self-designed major), Bass Connections (faculty-led research teams crossing departments), FOCUS (first-year living/learning clusters), Robertson Scholars (full-ride leadership program shared with UNC).

## Notre Dame [DdXRg64CcDP]

- **Read the diff before you write:** last cycle 3 of 5 short answers; this cycle 2 of 4 + the 150-word essay; the compliment prompt was cut; "what would you fight for" was kept. **A video portion was added last November** — the essays are no longer the only place they hear the applicant's voice.
- The 150-word essay = a why-Notre-Dame that never says the name: pick ONE factor Notre Dame does that most top schools do not (all four years in one residence hall with a rector living in it; no Greek life; a campus 81% Catholic; need-blind for every applicant on earth and no loans) and prove you actually need it. One factor beats a list — a list of five is your college list with the school names removed. Spend at least half on why the factor is yours; the reader checks whether you'd be happy here, not whether you researched here.
- **The three short answers map onto the three things the March decision release says it admits for:** faith prompt → "enthusiasm for the Catholic mission" (one decision faith changed; you don't have to be Catholic — about 1 in 5 enrolled students isn't; if not religious, name what you answer to and show one decision it changed — don't skip the prompt); service prompt → "commitment to serving others" (serve through the thing you're good at — tie it to the hook: if the file says applied math, the service is the school-budget or recycling data, not a generic food drive; a thing that kept working after you left — hours are not the point); fight-for prompt → "leadership" (one story tied to the hook, in a specific place, against a specific person or rule; you don't have to have won — trying to move a school board and losing reads more human than a cause you've never touched; the prompt is past tense in disguise).
- Pick the two where you have a real story, not the two that sound safest — two thin answers lose to one strong one every time. If you can only write one well, that's the one, and the community prompt is the second.
- REA by Nov 1: 11.8% (1,617 of 13,711) vs ~7% regular; not binding, but you cannot apply early to another private; 2,608 deferred — **a deferral at Notre Dame is a real second read, not a soft no**. Four essays ≈ 350 words total — the hook should show up in three of the four answers (they read them as one file, not four). Test optional this cycle, ends next year; ~60% of the last class submitted, middle half 1460–1540.
- *Draft-reach candidate that lost the final lock; kept because it is the cleanest example in the corpus of supplement prompts mapping one-to-one onto published institutional criteria — the same mapping method the locked list's supplement map uses.*

## How each school uses ED (the ED-card map) [DYFd5FmlpWR]

- **ED to the wrong school = throwing away your best card.** The test: "where am I a plausible admit that needs a small boost?" — where does ED tip you from maybe to yes?
- **UPenn, Duke — ED is the real admit round:** they fill ~50% of the class early; if unhooked and you want in, apply early. (Matches the per-school sections: UPenn 51% [DdPE50uiXaJ], Duke ~half [DadlabACXhj].)
- **Northwestern, Vanderbilt, WashU — ED is yield insurance:** they don't want to be the safety school (they're the choice safety for Ivy-rejected students); ED locks in truly committed students.
- **Brown and Dartmouth — ED is the ONLY round where unhooked applicants have a strong shot:** RD is brutal for these two (unhooked RD rate <4%); ED roughly doubles your odds. **This is the strongest external corroboration of the Dartmouth ED swap on the locked list** [DagOuYNidaX corroborates: ED ~17% vs RD ~3.6%, fills ~half the class].
- **Columbia and Cornell — ED matters less than you think:** both fill heavily with hooked applicants in ED; unhooked ED admits aren't dramatically higher than RD.
- **Harvard/Yale/Princeton — no ED, only REA:** non-binding but you can apply to only one private; the boost is marginal for unhooked applicants (a tiny boost to fence-sitters, very little to most). If cracking HYP is already a long shot, don't burn the ED card there.
- *This student: the Dartmouth ED bet passes the ED-card test on every version of this map (real admit round + the only unhooked shot + need-blind for internationals + 1540 above the Dartmouth median composite). The nuance to internalize: a deferral re-reads at RD where only ~5–10% of deferred Dartmouth applicants get in — the early bump does not carry over [DagOuYNidaX].*

## Need-blind: every claim has an asterisk [Dcv-h8GFlTh]

- Need-blind means ability to pay does not factor into the admit decision — it says nothing about whether aid gets fully funded, and nothing about every round of the process. ~100 US colleges call themselves need-blind; almost none mean it the same way.
- **Only 6 US colleges are truly need-blind for every applicant on Earth** (need blind + meet full need): Amherst, **Dartmouth**, Harvard, MIT, Princeton, Yale. *Flag for this student: Dartmouth being on this list is the load-bearing fact behind the ED bet at a $7.5k/yr budget — the family contribution can actually go to zero there.*
- The most common asterisk: need-blind = domestic only (Penn: US/Canada/Mexico, not international). NYU and Boston University say need-aware outright, even domestic. *Flag: the NYU RD fallback must be priced with need-aware mechanics in mind.*
- The policy usually ends at the waitlist: Reed admitted outright in 2008 (need-blind first round, need-aware once it turned to the waitlist). A former Penn officer: "It's not an official practice, but admissions officers are human." Full-pay status becomes a real advantage exactly where colleges stop publishing the rules.
- Undisclosed asterisks are scandal, not policy: 17 schools in the 568 Presidents Group (incl. Yale, Penn, Cornell, Georgetown) were sued for allegedly favoring wealthy/donor-connected applicants while claiming need-blind; twelve paid ~$320M in settlements; Georgetown's president chaired the group for over a decade.
- Plan around it: once the aid budget for a cycle is spent, ability to pay starts counting, disclosed or not — the shift happens at the waitlist, transfer admissions, and international pools. Before falling in love with "need blind," find out which version of the sentence your family is actually reading.
