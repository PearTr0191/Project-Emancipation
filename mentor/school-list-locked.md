## Early-round strategy (added 2026-09-01 stress test)

**Problem:** Stanford REA is the only early option, and it blocks ED at any other private school — so Dartmouth ED is off the table. Without the additions below, the early round is a single bet on one school, and if it rejects the entire cycle hinges on RD.

**Fix:** Michigan, Georgia Tech, and Purdue all offer **EA with a Nov 2 deadline (GT EA II) and Nov 1 (Michigan, Purdue) deadlines that are compatible with Stanford REA** (public universities; Stanford's REA restriction applies to private ED, not public EA). The Nov 1 slate is therefore:

| School | Deadline | Type | Compatible with Stanford REA? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Stanford** | Nov 1 | REA (restrictive) | — |
| **Michigan** | Nov 1 | EA | ✓ Yes |
| **Georgia Tech** | Nov 2 | EA II (non-GA residents) | ✓ Yes |
| **Purdue** | Nov 1 | EA (test-expected) | ✓ Yes |

3 applications due Nov 1 (Michigan, Purdue, Stanford) and 1 due Nov 2 (Georgia Tech EA II), with a realistic chance of an early yes from a target school before Stanford decides. The rest (Dartmouth, Northeastern, NYU, Case, RPI, Illinois Tech) go RD, due Jan 1–15.

**Note:** NYU offers ED I/ED II — ED I (Nov 10) is incompatible with Stanford REA, and ED II (Jan 1) is effectively RD. NYU's full-need Promise applies regardless of round, so there is no aid advantage to rushing ED. File NYU RD.

**Verified 2026-09-01:** Stanford REA explicitly permits public university EA applications (Stanford admissions site, Koppelman Group, Oriel Admissions — all confirm). Michigan EA (Nov 1, non-binding, May 1 commitment), Georgia Tech EA II (Nov 2, non-GA residents only, non-binding), and Purdue EA (Nov 1, non-binding) are all confirmed compatible. Georgia Tech has a two-track system: EA I (Oct 15, Georgia residents only) and EA II (Nov 2, non-Georgia residents) — the student files EA II as a non-Georgia resident international applicant. No further verification needed; the Nov 1 slate is confirmed.
