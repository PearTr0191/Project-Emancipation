> - **Michigan and Georgia Tech are re-added** — previously dropped on high remaining-balance risk. At $50k that risk is absorbable, and both are strong DS/AI fits.
> - **Northeastern's co-op income is now a real lever** — paid co-ops reduce net cost by an amount comparable to a mid-tier grant.
> - **Stanford and Dartmouth stay reaches** — the budget change does not move admit odds; it only removes the *financial* objection to applying.

Northeastern's co-op is the one structural advantage on the list that *generates* money rather than requesting it. Drexel provides the same mechanism at a lower sticker price as a backup.

## Early-round strategy (added 2026-09-01 stress test)

**Problem:** Stanford REA is the only early option, and it blocks ED at any other private school — so Dartmouth ED is off the table. Without the additions below, the early round is a single bet on one school, and if it rejects the entire cycle hinges on RD.

**Fix:** Michigan, Georgia Tech, and Purdue all offer **EA with a Nov 1 deadline that is compatible with Stanford REA** (public universities; Stanford's REA restriction applies to private ED, not public EA). The Nov 1 slate is therefore:

| Due Nov 1 | Type | Compatible with Stanford REA? |
|---|---|---|
| **Stanford** | REA (restrictive) | — |
| **Michigan** | EA | ✅ Yes |
| **Georgia Tech** | EA | ✅ Yes |
| **Purdue** | EA (test-expected) | ✅ Yes |

That's 4 applications due Nov 1 instead of 1, with a realistic chance of an early yes from a target school before Stanford decides. The rest (Dartmouth, Northeastern, NYU, Drexel, Case, RPI, ASU, Illinois Tech) go RD, due Jan 1–15.

**Note:** NYU offers ED I/ED II — ED I (Nov 10) is incompatible with Stanford REA, and ED II (Jan 1) is effectively RD. NYU's full-need Promise applies regardless of round, so there is no aid advantage to rushing ED. File NYU RD.

**Verified 2026-09-01:** Stanford REA explicitly permits public university EA applications (Stanford admissions site, Koppelman Group, Oriel Admissions — all confirm). Michigan EA (Nov 1, non-binding, May 1 commitment), Georgia Tech EA II (Nov 1, non-GA residents only, non-binding), and Purdue EA (Nov 1, non-binding) are all confirmed compatible. Georgia Tech has a two-track system: EA I (Oct 15, Georgia residents only) and EA II (Nov 1, non-Georgia residents) — the student files EA II as a non-Georgia resident international applicant. Drexel ED is binding and incompatible with Stanford REA — file Drexel RD. No further verification needed; the Nov 1 slate is confirmed.
